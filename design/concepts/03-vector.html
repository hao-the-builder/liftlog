Liftlog / design concept 03 of 03 · futuristic

Vector

Not chrome and not neon. The future this borrows from is optical: an instrument that draws with light on an empty field. Hairlines instead of borders, arcs instead of bars, type at 300 with air around it, and depth built from stacked translucent planes that each catch a rim of light along their top edge. A record does not change colour. It emits.

Palette

Almost no fills at all. Surfaces are white at 3 to 6 percent over a near-black void, so the palette is really one colour at eight opacities. That is the discipline that keeps this from becoming the purple-glass look every generated interface reaches for.

Type

Space Grotesk at 300, which is the lightest weight any of the three concepts uses and the thing that most changes the feel: wide, calm, slightly technical. Labels get 0.2em tracking, far more than the other two, because generous letter-spacing on small caps is what reads as instrumentation rather than as a web app.

The four decisions

The same four questions, answered with light instead of pigment or surface.

1 — What carries the primary action?

The only solid object in a field of outlines.

A white pill at 20.16:1 with a soft outer bloom. Everywhere else is a 1px hairline over a 3 percent wash, so solidity itself becomes the signal. You do not have to look for the button; it is the only thing that is actually there.

2 — How does a record feel like a record?

The row emits. Nothing fills and nothing inverts.

Two bright hairlines bracket the row, the value itself picks up a diffraction bloom, and the check throws a wide halo. It is the quietest of the three answers and the most distinctive, because glow in this concept is rationed to exactly two things: the running timer and a record. Earned, not sprayed.

3 — What do the charts encode with?

Outline, silver, and emitting white.

#878E95 in the band and pure white for the current week, 3.32:1 apart, with the under-band bar reduced to a hairline outline for the same reason as the metallic concept. The line chart is 1.5px rather than 2px, and the live point gets a thin concentric ring instead of a larger dot, which is how an instrument marks a reading.

4 — Where does danger go?

A deep red pill, the one saturated thing in the system.

#B8241A with a white label at 6.35:1. Deeper than the reds in the other concepts, because against a void with no other colour in it a bright red reads as an alarm going off rather than as a button. It keeps its own faint bloom so it belongs to the same physics as everything else.

What this costs you

backdrop-filter on every pane is the most expensive thing any of these three asks of a phone GPU, and it is the one that would need measuring against the 90-plus Lighthouse score before committing. Light weights at 300 also need testing on a real screen in a dim room, where thin white type on near-black can shimmer.

Rejected

Named so they do not creep back in during implementation.

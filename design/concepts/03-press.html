Liftlog — design concept 03 / 03 Light substrate

Press

The one that turns the lights on. A training log is a printed document: unbleached stock, carbon ink, oversized numerals set like a plate stamp. Black, silver and white taken the other way round, because nothing in the brief said the black had to be the background.

Palette

Never pure white paper, and never pure black ink. #F4F4F0 is matte documentation stock, warm enough to stop the screen glaring in a bright gym. Ink is #0A0A0A, which carries 17.96:1 and still reads as a material rather than a void.

Type

Archivo Black for every number that matters and every screen title, at a scale that would be absurd anywhere except on a plate. Archivo regular for language, IBM Plex Mono for metadata and table columns. Three roles, no overlap.

Screens

One more argument for this direction that has nothing to do with taste: a light substrate is readable in direct sunlight. If you ever train outdoors or in a gym with a glass wall, the dark concepts lose and this one does not.

The four decisions

The same four questions, answered from the other side of the substrate.

1 — What carries the primary action?

Solid ink on paper.

A black button with a white label, 17.96:1, square-ish at 0px radius. On a light substrate the darkest element is automatically the heaviest, so the hierarchy needs no colour at all. This is the one place the design is simpler than the dark concepts rather than harder.

2 — How does a record feel like a record?

The row is struck in solid ink and reversed out.

A black band across a white page is the loudest mark available in print, and it is the exact inverse of the Plate and Telemetry answer, which makes it just as rare and just as unmistakable. A bordered Record tab breaks the top edge, like a tab stuck onto a page.

3 — What do the charts encode with?

Ink, silver, and pale stock.

#D2D2CC under the band, #8E8E88 in it, solid ink for the current week. Hairline gridlines in #DEDDD8 with a solid ink baseline, because the axis is structure and the grid is reference, and they should not weigh the same. No dashed lines anywhere.

4 — Where does danger go?

Hazard red, one control, fill only.

#D62828 with a white label at 5.01:1. The Swiss print tradition this borrows from has always used a single aviation red against black and paper, so it is native to the direction rather than an exception carved out of it.

What this costs you

A bright screen in a dark gym at 6am, and a rebuild of the PWA theme colour, splash screen and every icon, which are all currently drawn for a near-black shell. It is the biggest implementation job of the three and the most complete change of character.

Rejected

Named so they do not creep back in during implementation.

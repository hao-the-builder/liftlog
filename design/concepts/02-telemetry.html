Liftlog — design concept 02 / 03 Rev 1.0

Tele­metry

A training log is instrumentation. This direction stops pretending otherwise: monospace as the primary voice, visible compartments instead of floating cards, every corner at ninety degrees, and metadata left on display the way a readout leaves it on display. One accent, hazard red, and it only ever means this cannot be undone.

Palette

A deactivated CRT, not a black hole. #0A0A0A rather than pure black so the panels above it have somewhere to sit. Red appears on exactly one control in the entire app and is never used for text, only as a fill.

Type

IBM Plex Mono runs the interface. Archivo at 800 appears only as structural headers, uppercase, tracked tight enough that the glyphs form a solid block. Nothing is set in a proportional face at small sizes, which is the opposite of the current app and the single biggest change you would feel.

The razor-thin dividers are gap: 1px on a grid over a contrasting parent, not borders. It gives mathematically exact hairlines with no double-border seams where compartments meet.

The four decisions

The same four questions every monochrome system has to answer.

1 — What carries the primary action?

White fill, square, uppercase, tracked.

19.80:1 against the substrate and the only filled element in view. It reads as a hardware key rather than a web button, which is the point: you are pressing a control, not clicking a link.

2 — How does a record feel like a record?

The whole row inverts and takes a stamp.

A white band across a dark readout is how instrumentation has always flagged an out-of-range value. The Record stamp sits in the corner in reversed type, square, no bloom. Restrained compared to the magenta bloom, and it survives being glanced at from two metres away with a bar on your back.

3 — What do the charts encode with?

Three measured greys, square marks, visible axes.

#3A3A3A under the band, #8A8A8A in it, white for the current week, adjacent pairs clearing 3:1. Markers are squares rather than circles and the line has no curve smoothing, because a smoothed line invents data between two weigh-ins that did not happen.

4 — Where does danger go?

Hazard red, one control, fill only.

#E61919 with a white label at 4.65:1. It appears on destructive confirms and nowhere else in the app, which is the same discipline the current design applies to magenta. Red is never used as text colour, never on a border, never on a chart.

What this costs you

Uppercase monospace at 11px is dense and technical. It is excellent for scanning numbers and worse for reading sentences, so the progression hints and empty states get terser. If you want the app to feel warm, this is not the one.

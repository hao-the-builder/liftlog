Liftlog / design concept 01 of 03

Plate

Silver is not a colour here, it is a material. Stamped numerals on cast iron, the milled face of a dumbbell handle, the knurl under chalk. The accent is pure white, because in a monochrome system the brightest thing on the screen should be the thing you are meant to press.

Palette

Six neutrals and one white. Never #000000: it crushes OLED detail and kills the only light this design has to spend. Every grey is cooled by two points of green so the stack reads as steel rather than as a browser default.

Type

Archivo for language, JetBrains Mono for every number you read or edit. The split is not decorative: a monospaced digit physically cannot change width, so a countdown and a rep edit never make the row twitch.

The four decisions

Dropping hue forces four questions that the current volt-and-magenta palette answered for free. This is how Plate answers them.

1 — What carries the primary action?

Pure white, and only on one element per screen.

White against #0A0B0B is 19.71:1, the largest contrast step available. It needs no hue to out-shout anything else, and because it is the extreme of the scale, there is no argument about which element is primary: the one with light on it.

2 — How does a record feel like a record?

The row inverts. Black type on a white field.

Magenta earned its meaning by appearing nowhere else. Inversion earns it the same way: nothing else in the app flips polarity, so when a row goes white it is unmistakable even in peripheral vision, which is where you are looking when you rack a bar. A small black Record stamp names it, and the whole thing animates once at 320ms.

3 — What do the charts encode with?

Lightness, in three steps, with white reserved for now.

Three measured steps: #3A3C3C under the band, #8A8E8F inside it, #FFFFFF for the current week. The obvious ramp, silver straight to white, failed: those two sit 1.91:1 apart and read as one colour on a phone. Re-stepped, adjacent pairs clear 3.36:1 and 3.31:1. One line, one axis, no dual-axis anything.

4 — Where does danger go?

Nowhere. Destructive actions are white too, and spell it out.

This is the strict reading of black, silver and white: no red at all. A delete gets the same white fill as any primary action, and the safety comes from the sheet, the explicit verb and the fact that it is never the default. Honest about the cost: colour-coded danger is a real affordance and this concept gives it up on purpose.

The tradeoff to weigh

You asked for the delete button to be red an hour ago. Plate says no, and that is the one thing to decide before I build it. Concepts 02 and 03 both keep a single hazard red, restricted to destructive confirms and nothing else.
